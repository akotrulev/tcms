## 1. Three corrections to the brief's premises

The survey that preceded this plan was right about the data model and the shape of the gaps. Three
of its framing statements do not survive reading the code, and two of them change the answer.

### 1.1 The people/role **reads** are already open to every member. It is the **writes** that are admin-only.

The brief says *"every people/role read today is gated: `company:members` and `company:teams` are
Company-Admin-only"*. The permissions are indeed Company-Admin-only — but they gate the writes, not
the reads.

| Endpoint | Actual gate | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `GET /users` | `company:read` | `AdminDirectoryService.java:179` (`if (!reach.readsCompany()) return List.of()`) |
| `GET /teams` | `company:read` | `AdminDirectoryService.java:249` |
| `GET /roles` | `company:read` | `AdminDirectoryService.java:326` |
| `GET /role-assignments` | `company:read`, then per-row `mayStaff` | `AdminDirectoryService.java:404` and `:421` |
| `POST/DELETE /users` (invite, remove) | `company:members` | `AdminDirectoryService.java:62`, `AdminController.java:83,113` |
| all five `/teams` writes | `company:teams` | `TeamController.java:67-120`, `AdminReach.java:33-41` |

`company:read` is held by **every member**, explicitly, through the non-assignable `Company Member`
baseline role `V15` seeds on joining (`AdminDirectoryService.java:109-126`, `AdminReach.java:49-56`).
So a plain member today can already read the whole directory — names, emails, avatars, statuses,
every team with its leads and members, and each team's project chips clipped to the projects that
member can see.

**Consequence for this plan:** §6k's *"readable by any authenticated member of that company"* is not
a new position to argue for. It is **the position already implemented**, and the org chart inherits
it rather than establishing it. An admin-only org chart would be a *regression* against shipped
behaviour, not a conservative choice.

### 1.2 The precedent for "the org chart must not be a way around project visibility" is already in the code, and it is a project-axis filter

`AdminDirectoryService.java:303-311`, in the team renderer:

```java
grantsByTeam.getOrDefault(team.getId(), List.of()).stream()
        // A team can hold a grant on a project this reader has none on.
        // Rendering its name here would make the org chart a way around
        // "membership conveys zero project visibility" (roles spec §A1).
        .filter(grant -> reach.maySee(grant.getProjectId()))
```

`maySee` is `visibleProjects.contains(projectId)`, i.e. `project:read`
(`AdminReach.java:86-88`, populated at `AdminDirectoryService.java:140-147`). The comment names this
feature by name. **The rule this plan needs already exists and is already enforced on the closest
analogue.** §5's design is that rule applied to a second axis, not a new rule.

`AdminReach.java:26-28` states the underlying distinction the whole design rests on: *"an id the
caller already holds is not a disclosure, a name is (§6t)."*

### 1.3 §84 already decided that the org chart is gated on nothing, and recorded why inventing a key would be wrong

`frontend/src/features/board/TeamBoardPage.tsx:27-40`:

> `/admin/teams` is the company administration screen and its every affordance is gated on
> `company:teams`; this board is gated on **nothing at all**. Any authenticated member of the
> company may open any team's board — §6k's rule that the org chart itself is readable by every
> member, and §84's finding that there is no permission key to gate it on and inventing one would be
> a 403 no administrator could grant their way out of.

And, four paragraphs down (`:43-46`): *"The bound is the READER's, and this screen must not pretend
otherwise… two people looking at the same team board legitimately see different cards."*

**That is this plan's design, already taken as a decision on an adjacent screen.** §5 follows it
rather than re-deciding it, and §9's frontend section follows its routing consequence too (a screen
every member may open must not live under an `/admin` prefix).

## 2. Scope

### In

1. A **read-only assignment matrix** for one company: people × the projects the viewer can see,
   each cell carrying the person's effective PROJECT-scope role there and how it is held.
2. The **team structure** beside it — the existing `parent_team_id` tree, leads and members —
   reusing the existing `Team` contract schema unchanged.
3. **Company-scope badges** on a person (Company Admin), rendered on the person rather than
   smeared across every column, per roles spec §A1.1 property 2.
4. **Vacancy surfacing** (§6k's *"the reason someone opens an org chart in the first place"*):
   people in no team, teams with no lead, people with no project assignment at all.
5. One new endpoint, its contract, its tests, its route, its rail entry, and the plain-language
   record.

### Out — explicitly, each with who owns it if it is ever wanted

| Out of scope | Why | Who owns it if asked for |
|---|---|---|
| **Reporting hierarchy / manager / reports-to** | The user chose an assignment matrix. There is no `manager_id`, `reports_to`, job title, seniority, start date or allocation column anywhere (verified against `V1__initial_schema.sql:130-157` for `team`/`team_membership` and `:104` for `company_membership`). Adding one is a data-model decision, a migration, a write surface and an ownership question about who keeps it current. | a future slice, `data-architect` + `be-java` |

## 4. The central decision — what may a plain member see?

### 4.1 What §6k says

`docs/plans/2026-07-30-tcms-high-level-plan.md:1105`:

> **Permissions** — `company:teams` to edit structure; the chart itself is readable by any
> authenticated **member of that company**. Org structure is not sensitive *within* a company, and
> hiding it defeats the purpose — but it is absolutely sensitive across companies, which is the one
> place the "readable by anyone" rule is narrowed.

And `:1090`, on what a node shows:

> **Projects the team holds roles on**, with the role — the single most useful piece of information
> on the chart, since it answers "who owns this project?" in one glance.

### 4.2 What the access model says, and where the two meet

Roles spec §A1: *"Company membership grants authentication into a company and zero project
visibility. A regular user sees only the projects they hold a grant on — not the company's project
list, not names in a picker, not counts on a dashboard."*

These two are not in conflict; they constrain **different axes**. §6k is about the **people** axis
(who is here, in which team) and §A1 is about the **project** axis (which engagements exist). The
existing team renderer already resolves them exactly that way (§1.2).

### 4.3 The options

**Option A — admin-only chart.** Gate the whole surface on `company:members`.

- Contradicts §6k directly, and contradicts §84's recorded finding (§1.3).
- Would be a **regression**: a plain member already reads the whole team structure today.
- Makes the chart useless for its stated purpose. §6k opens *"which makes a lead actually open it"*;
  a lead who is not a Company Admin holds neither `company:members` nor `company:teams`.
- **Reject.**

**Option B — names and teams to everyone; roles and projects admin-only.**

- Also a regression: `Team.projects[]` already ships `{projectId, projectName, roleName}` to every
  member, clipped to their visible projects (`openapi.yaml:10589-10600`,
  `AdminDirectoryService.java:307-311`).
- Removes the thing §6k calls *"the single most useful piece of information on the chart."*
- **Reject.**

**Option C — everyone sees the whole people axis; the project axis is clipped to the viewer's own
`project:read`, and administrative facts are redacted per field.** *(recommended)*

- Person rows: complete. Every non-`REMOVED` member of the company, their teams, their lead flags,
  their status. This is what `GET /users` and `GET /teams` already serve.
- Project columns: **only the viewer's `visibleProjects`**. A project the viewer cannot open
  contributes no column, no cell, no count, and no placeholder.
- Administrative facts stay redacted by the existing per-field rule: `lastLoginAt` remains null for
  a caller without `company:members` (`UserView.java:21-25`, applied at
  `AdminDirectoryService.java:240-241`).
- Grant ids and revocability are **absent from this surface entirely** — the chart is not a write
  surface, and `GET /role-assignments` remains the one place a revocable row appears.
- Consistent with §6k, §A1, §84, and the shipped `Team.projects[]` filter.
- **Recommend.**

**Option C2 — Option C, but role *names* only on projects where the viewer holds
`project:members`; elsewhere a membership dot with no role.**

- Preserves the administer-filter's spirit one axis further.
- Costs the chart its point for every reader below Test Lead, which is most readers.
- Presented because it is the one honest narrowing of C, and it is **Q1 in §14** — a disclosure
  decision the user should own rather than one engineering should assume.

**Option D — show a count of projects the viewer cannot see ("+3 more").**

- A count is a disclosure. §6t requires an unreachable project and a non-existent one to be
  indistinguishable; `AdminReach.java:26-28` draws the same line (*"an id the caller already holds is
  not a disclosure, a name is"*), and a count is a name-free existence claim about the company's
  portfolio.
- **Reject.** The UI states the rule unconditionally instead — see §9.4.

### 4.4 The redaction table (Option C)

Per field, per viewer tier. "Every member" means `company:read`, held by all.

| Field | Every member | `project:read` on that project | `company:members` | Rule | Precedent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `id`, `displayName`, `avatarUrl` | ✓ | — | ✓ | unredacted | already on the wire, `AdminDirectoryService.java:493-497` |
| `email` | ✓ | — | ✓ | unredacted | `UserRef` requires it (`openapi.yaml:6122-6128`); already served to every member |
| `status` (`ACTIVE`/`INVITED`/`DEACTIVATED`) | ✓ | — | ✓ | unredacted | `AdminDirectoryService.java:158-173`, `:539-544` |
| `teams[]` (id, name) + `isLead` | ✓ | — | ✓ | unredacted | `Team.leads` / `Team.members` already public to members (`AdminDirectoryService.java:296-302`) |
| **a matrix cell** (`projectId`, `roleName`, `via`, `viaTeam`) | — | ✓ | ✓ | **cell exists only if the *viewer* holds `project:read` on that project.** No cell, no count, no placeholder otherwise | `AdminDirectoryService.java:303-311`; `AdminReach.java:86-88`; §6t |
| `companyRoles[]` (e.g. `Company Admin`) | ✓ | — | ✓ | unredacted — it is a claim about the company, and the company's administrators are not a secret from its members | roles spec §A1.1 property 2; **[inferred]** — no existing endpoint serves this fact, so it is new. See Q2 |
| `lastLoginAt` | **null** | — | ✓ | withheld | `UserView.java:21-25`, the brief's own named precedent |
| grant `id` / "may I revoke this" | absent | absent | absent | not on this surface at all | `AdminDirectoryService.java:384-395` — that invariant belongs to `GET /role-assignments` and stays there |
| SYSTEM-scope (is this person an Instance Admin) | absent | absent | absent | not exposed | `instance_role_assignment` has no `company_id` and no RLS (`V1:303-315`). Serving it from a company surface would put an instance fact on a tenant's wire. See Q3 |

**The rule the whole table reduces to:** *the people axis is a company fact; the project axis is the
reader's own.* Two colleagues open the same chart, see the same people, and see different matrices.
That is the design (§1.3, and §84's "the bound is the READER's"), and §9.4 requires the screen to
**say so** rather than render a sparser grid in silence.

## 5. The read surface

### 5.1 New path, not an extension of `GET /users`

`GET /api/v1/org-chart` → `200 OrgChart`. Gate `company:read`; a caller who somehow fails it gets
`{teams: [], people: [], projects: []}` rather than a 403, for the reason
`AdminDirectoryService.java:32-42` states: the contract declares only 200 on the identity reads, and
inventing a status a generated client does not know about is the costlier divergence. Only `200` is
declared.

**Why not extend `GET /users` with a `projects[]` field** (the obvious cheap move):

1. `User` has three other consumers — the grant picker, the invite/remove flow, and both TypeScript
   stacks' generated types — none of which wants a per-caller project matrix. A changed `User` is a
   changed `schema.d.ts` in `frontend/` **and** `packages/client/`, plus `contractExchange`/
   `contractBodies` fixtures in both.
2. The matrix needs teams, people, projects and cells to come from **one snapshot**. Three queries
   assembled client-side render a grid whose axes were read at three different moments.
3. `User.teams` is `string[]` (names only, `openapi.yaml:10392-10394`). The chart must link a person
   to a team **node**, which needs ids. Widening that field is a breaking change to a shipped shape;
   a new schema is not.

**Consequence, stated plainly: this slice changes no existing schema.** `npm run gen:api` must still
run in both `frontend/` and `packages/client/` (`schemaFreshness.test.ts` and the byte-identical-copy
rule), but **no existing generated type moves**, so no existing test fixture or consumer is touched.
That is the single largest risk this shape removes.

### 5.2 Response shapes (three new schemas, one new path)

```yaml

  type: object
  required: [teams, people, projects]
  properties:
    teams:
      type: array
      items: { $ref: '#/components/schemas/Team' }      # EXISTING schema, unchanged
    people:
      type: array
      items: { $ref: '#/components/schemas/OrgChartPerson' }
    projects:
      type: array
      items: { $ref: '#/components/schemas/OrgChartProject' }

        would be a name-free existence claim about the company's portfolio.

        companyRoles:
          type: array
          items: { type: string }

            **Null for a caller who does not hold `company:members`** — the same rule and the

  type: object
  required: [projectId, roleName, via]
  properties:
    projectId: { type: string, format: uuid }

      type: string

        The person's EFFECTIVE PROJECT-scope role here, after roles spec §A1.1's
```

`required` does not descend, and without it every generated property is optional and no consumer can
use one. That is why `OrgChartProject` and `OrgChartAssignment` are named schemas rather than inline
objects.

**`operationId`:** none, matching its neighbours. The brief is right that none of the identity paths
carry one (39 exist in the file, none of them here). Adding one to a single new path in that family
would make the family inconsistent to buy nothing. **[inferred — this is a convention call, flagged
as Q5 in §14 in case the `packages/` generator has a preference.]**

## 6. Does this need a migration? — argued both ways, answer: **no**

### The case for a migration

1. **A `sort_order` on `team`** so the chart's layout is stable and author-controlled rather than
   alphabetical.
   *Rejected.* `TeamTree` already sorts by name (`TeamsPage.tsx:404`) and
   `teams.findAllByOrderByNameAsc()` already returns them that way
   (`AdminDirectoryService.java:252`). Alphabetical **is** stable. A manual ordering is a write
   surface, a drag affordance and a per-company migration for a cosmetic gain.
2. **A materialised view or a denormalised `person_project_role` table** for matrix performance.
   *Rejected.* §8's design resolves the entire matrix in **two queries for the whole company**, not
   N×M. A cache that can go stale is exactly the shape §6l's central rule refuses and the
   permission model's invalidation list (`V1:283-287`) already shows to be non-trivial — five tables
   invalidate a grant.
3. **An index** to make the two IN-list queries cheap.
   ~~*Probably rejected, and it is `data-architect`'s call (task T5).*~~ **→ half right, and the
   half it got wrong is the one that mattered. T5 measured it; see §6.1.** `role_assignment_user_idx
   (company_id, user_id, project_id)` and `role_assignment_team_idx (company_id, team_id,
   project_id)` already exist (`V1:288-289`), and both queries filter on `company_id` plus
   `user_id IN (…)` / `team_id IN (…)` — the leading two columns of each. ~~**[inferred: an index is
   probably unnecessary; this is a plan asserting a requirement, and `EXPLAIN` against a realistic
   company is the evidence, which is why T5 exists rather than a claim here.]**~~ **→ superseded by
   §6.1: no `role_assignment` index is wanted, and an index IS wanted — on `team_membership`, the
   table this paragraph never looked at, because the TEAM arm of each query reaches the person
   through `team_membership.user_id` and nothing indexes that column.**

### The case against, which wins

Everything the chart renders is derivable from tables that already exist and already carry
`company_id` and RLS: `company_membership`, `team`, `team_membership`, `role_assignment`, `role`,
`role_permission`, `project`, `app_user`. The brief's own constraint — *prefer reusing what is there
over adding tables* — and the excluded reporting hierarchy together remove the only thing that would
have needed a column.

**If T5 concludes an index is wanted:** the Flyway version is **reserved at integration time, in
landing order, not now** — `out-of-order` is unset, so a `V53` landing after a `V54` is refused on
any already-migrated database, and Testcontainers cannot see it because it migrates a fresh
container every time (`docs/plans/2026-07-31-parallel-execution-rework.md`, and CLAUDE.md's
concurrency section). The head today is `V52__the_tier_line_is_approvals_and_configuration.sql`.

**The head named in the sentence above is stale** — it has moved on since this section was written,
and as of 2026-08-17 it is `V66__report_generation_takes_an_idempotency_key.sql`. That changes
nothing about the rule: **the version for the DDL in §6.1 stays unassigned**, and is taken at
integration in landing order. Do not read a number out of either sentence.

### 6.1 T5's answer, measured: **an index IS wanted — and not on `role_assignment`**

**Verdict.** The two `role_assignment` indexes this section asked about are adequate and no third is
wanted. But the three matrix queries do not reach a person only through `role_assignment.user_id` —
their **TEAM arm reaches them through `team_membership.user_id`**, and `team_membership` has exactly
two indexes: its primary key `(team_id, user_id)` and nothing else. `user_id` is the *trailing*
column of that key and `company_id` is in no index at all, so every one of those arms **sequentially
scans the whole instance's `team_membership`** — every other client company's rows included. One
index closes it:

**The index below LANDED as `V67__team_membership_answers_which_teams_a_person_is_in.sql`
(2026-08-17), and the DDL was taken in the PLAIN form — the `CONCURRENTLY` line and the
`flyway:no-transaction` hatch below are ~~recommended~~ → superseded.** The column list, the index
name, the two-vs-three-column choice and every measurement in this section stand unchanged; only the
build mode was reversed. The reason is in `V67`'s header at length and in one sentence here: an
`ACCESS EXCLUSIVE` lock costs its **duration**, duration is a function of **table size**, and
read-frequency changes only how many requests wait out those milliseconds. `team_membership` is
read-hot and physically tiny (8.5 MB at the synthetic ceiling, 14 pages at the twenty-company shape),
Flyway runs pre-deploy rather than at app startup, and taking `CONCURRENTLY` would have made this the
repo's **first** no-transaction migration — buying an `INVALID`-index recovery path and a
non-rollback-able failed history, against a six-migration precedent (`V17`, `V42`, `V59`, `V62` on
the record; `V11` and `V14` by the same call unstated), to avoid a blip inside a deploy window.
Deciding it for one index would also not be `V17`'s *"a decision for all four at once"*; when that
decision is due it wants its own slice.

#### How this was measured, so the numbers can be re-derived rather than believed

- A throwaway `postgres:17` container (**PostgreSQL 17.10**, the same major as compose and the
  Testcontainers lane), not the `tcms` stack — a distinct container name, no compose project.
- `src/test/resources/db/testinit/tcms-roles.sql` first, so `tcms_owner` is a **non-superuser** owner
  and `tcms_app` owns nothing and holds no `BYPASSRLS` — the deployment's ordering (initdb → Flyway).
- All **66 migrations** applied in version order as `tcms_owner`, `ON_ERROR_STOP`, all clean. So
  `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` and V4's `nullif(…, '')` predicate are live on all 56 policies.
- **Every plan below was taken as `tcms_app`** with `app.current_company` set to the target company,
  which is the query production runs: the policy is part of it. The same plans taken as `tcms_owner`
  are **byte-identical in shape and within noise on time** (Q1 6.298 ms vs 6.329 ms) — which is
  itself the evidence that `FORCE` is doing its job, since a plain `ENABLE` would have let the owner
  out. Taken as the container **superuser** the `One-Time Filter` node carrying the policy predicate
  **disappears from the plan entirely**; that is the "measuring a different query" trap, and it is
  why nothing here was measured as one.
- Seeded with a set-based generator, then `ANALYZE`. Plans were run twice and the second reported, so
  `Buffers` read as `hit` and cache warm-up is not being mistaken for work.

**Sizes.** The instance: **1,000 companies · 40,020 users · 40,020 company memberships · 6,004

#### Honest bounds on the claim

- **The defect is latent, and its size is a function of the instance rather than the company.**
  Re-measured with `team_membership` cut to a 20-company instance (**1,230 rows, 14 pages**),
  still without the index: the same seq scan node costs **0.062 ms**, single-member Q1 is 0.436 ms
  and 60-member Q1 is 6.735 ms — indistinguishable from the indexed 1,000-company figure. So
  production today, at one company, cannot feel this, nothing that has shipped is slow because of it,
  and **that is the argument for landing the index while it is free rather than the argument against
  it**: the cost arrives with the second and hundredth client, which is the thing this product is
  for.
- **A three-column variant was measured and rejected.** `(company_id, user_id, team_id)` earns an
  `Index Only Scan` and 6.771 ms on Q1 — noise-identical to the two-column index's 6.4 ms — for
  **4000 kB instead of 2312 kB**. `INCLUDE` has the same problem. The two-column form wins, and
  `is_lead`/`joined_at` readers keep their heap visit.
- **The write cost is negligible and worth stating rather than assuming**: `team_membership` is
  written only by `TeamWriteService`'s membership puts and removes — an administrative action, not a
  request-path write — so the one-index tax on INSERT/DELETE buys a read win on every permission
  check.
- **What was not measured:** concurrency, HikariCP behaviour, the SPA end-to-end, and the reverse
  question of whether `team_membership_pkey` is still earning its keep (it is — `findByTeamId` and
  the team board read that direction). No `pg_stat_statements` fleet ranking, because there is no
  fleet: this is a synthetic instance, and the claim it supports is about a plan node, not about
  which query annoys a user most.
- **This finding adds no permission, no table and no column**, so `SchemaConventionTest`'s
  `company_id`-on-every-table arm and the ERD §0.1 exception list are untouched.

## 7. Where the team→project→person resolution belongs: **the server**

### 7.1 The argument, and it is not a preference

**The client cannot compute this at all, even in principle, for the readers the chart is for.**
The only company-wide grant list on the wire is `GET /role-assignments`, and it is filtered to
`reach.mayStaff` (`AdminDirectoryService.java:421`) — the grants the caller could **revoke**. A
plain member holds `project:members` nowhere, so they receive `[]`. Under Option C the org chart is
for exactly those people. There is no input.

That alone settles it. Three further arguments, each from this codebase's own recorded findings:

1. **`ProjectMembershipService`'s header (`:48-58`) is this exact decision, already taken:**

   > A grant reaches a person directly, through a team, or company-wide, and a query over
   > `role_assignment WHERE project_id = ?` finds only the first. It would silently omit every person
   > staffed through a team — the shape the org chart exists to encourage — and omit every Company
   > Admin… More than a completeness bug, it is the second authorization-resolution path plan §6b
   > forbids.

   It names the org chart. Building the chart's resolution on the client would be that defect,
   deliberately, in the feature the sentence was written about.

2. **`roleAccess.ts` already says of itself that it is a second implementation** (`:26-29`):

   > That is the same sentence the backend's `hasProjectScopedAssignment` is written under, and it is
   > repeated here because this is the second implementation of the rule, not because it is obvious.

   A third would be the one nobody keeps in step. §A1.1's replace-don't-union rule currently exists
   in **two** Java places already — `PermissionEvaluator.projectTerm` (`:189-193`, for permissions)
   and `ProjectService.view` (`:586`, for the displayed role name) — plus `roleAccess.ts`. See §8.3.

3. **The server already has every primitive.** `PermissionEvaluator.evaluate(userId, projectId)`
   takes **any** `userId`, not only the caller (`:121`); `ProjectAccess.canView(userId, projectId)`
   likewise (`ProjectAccess.java:44`, used per-person at `ProjectMembershipService.java:110`);
   `RoleAssignmentRepository.findProjectRoleNames` / `findInheritedRoleNames` (`:240`, `:270`) are
   already whole-company, single-query, per-user resolutions of the display role.

### 7.2 …and `roleAccess.ts` stays, unchanged

**Do not delete it, and do not feed the org chart from it.** It answers a genuinely different
question on a different screen: `ProjectAccessPage` renders **grants an administrator may revoke**
(rows), and `GET /role-assignments` is the correct input for that. The org chart renders **effective
roles** (cells) for a reader who may revoke nothing.

A follow-up worth naming and **not** doing here: once the server answers effective roles,
`ProjectAccessPage` could read that answer and `roleAccess.ts`'s override arithmetic could go. That
is a refactor of a shipped screen with its own tests and its own §31 disable-don't-hide behaviour,
and folding it into a new feature is how a read-only slice acquires a regression surface. Recorded
in §14 Q4.

## 8. The server design

### 8.1 Module, controller, service

- **Module:** `identity`. It owns people, teams, roles and grants already.
- **Controller:** a new `OrgChartController` mapping `/api/v1/org-chart`. One new path family, one
  controller — `TeamController.java:25-32`'s stated rule (*"One path family, one controller"*), and
  the reason `/teams` moved off `AdminController` in the first place.
- **Service:** a new `OrgChartService`, **reusing** rather than re-implementing:
  - `AdminDirectoryService.reachOf(userId)` for the project axis (`:128-152`);
  - `AdminDirectoryService.peopleById()` for `UserRef` assembly — package-private and already shared
    with `ProjectMembershipService` for precisely this reason (`:475-481`): it is the one place the
    §6u display-name rule (name from `company_membership`, not `app_user.canonical_name`) is
    applied, and no architecture test can see a second implementation of it;
  - `AdminDirectoryService.listTeams(userId)` for the team layer, so the chart and `/admin/teams`
    cannot draw different trees or different project chips.

  `AdminDirectoryService` is 545 lines and is already *the* assembler; a new service that calls into
  it beats a sixth read method on it. **[inferred — a judgment call about file size, not a rule.]**

### 8.2 Two queries for the whole matrix, not N×M

The naive shape is one `PermissionEvaluator.evaluate(person, project)` per pair. For 40 people and 8
projects that is 320 evaluations, several queries each. `AdminDirectoryService.reachOf` and
`ProjectMembershipService.membersOf` both accept an N-shaped cost with a stated ceiling
(`:100-107`, `:95-102`) — but N×M is not N, and neither header licenses it.

**The shape that avoids it entirely:** the two existing role-name queries are already whole-company
per user and already union direct + team grants in SQL
(`RoleAssignmentRepository.java:225-241`, `:255-271`). Generalise each from `:userId` to a
**`:userIds` IN-list**, and the whole matrix is:

- **query 1** — every project-naming grant for every member: `(userId, projectId, roleName,
  permissionCount, via, teamId)`;
- **query 2** — every company-wide PROJECT-scope grant for every member: `(userId, roleName,
  permissionCount)` — the inherited default;
- **query 3** — COMPANY-scope role names per member, for `companyRoles`. **[inferred: this is a new
  projection; `findCompanyRoleHeldPermissions` returns permission rows, not role names.]**

Two extra columns on query 1 carry `via`:
`CASE WHEN ra.user_id = :userId THEN 'DIRECT' ELSE 'TEAM' END` and `ra.team_id`. Additive; the
existing `ProjectRoleName` projection interface gains two accessors and `ProjectView` ignores them.

**Have `ProjectService` read the same generalised queries with a single-element list.** Then there is
one query family, `Project.myRole` keeps working by construction, and a change to the resolution
cannot reach one caller and miss the other. **This is the risky half of the slice** — it touches a
caller-facing field on a shipped screen — so T2 is gated by the existing `myRole` tests and is the
one task that must not be bundled with anything else.

**Ranking:** where a person holds two grants on one project (directly and through a team, or through
two teams), the contract has room for one string and the winner is the one whose role confers more
permissions — **derived from `role_permission`, never a hard-coded name ordering**. That rule and
its reason are already written at `RoleAssignmentRepository.java:215-223`, and generalising the query
must carry it (now a max **per (user, project)** rather than per project).

### 8.3 The §A1.1 override, and the third implementation this must not become

The display-side override is one line today, at `ProjectService.java:586`:

```java
roleNames.getOrDefault(project.getId(), inheritedRoleName)
```

*a grant naming the project wins; otherwise the inherited role; null when neither.* The org chart
needs the same rule per person.

**Extract it into one named, tested helper** that both `ProjectService.view` and `OrgChartService`
call — an `EffectiveRoleName` (or similar) in `identity`, taking the person's project-naming grants
and their inherited grant and answering the cell. Copying the line is how the two disagree the first
time somebody touches §A1.1; and the file that would notice is `PermissionEvaluator`, which is a
different resolution (permissions, not names) and would not.

**And it needs a test that pins it to the authorization answer**, because the display path and the
permission path are two resolutions of one rule and only one of them is the truth. Name it now:

> For every (person, project) cell the chart serves, `ProjectAccess.canView(person, project)` must be
> **true** — a chart cell asserting somebody has a role on a project they cannot open is the display
> path disagreeing with the evaluator. And for every (person, project) where `canView` is true and
> the person holds no COMPANY-scope role, a cell must **exist** — the other direction, which is the
> one that hides people.

That is `qa-api-java`'s T4 and it is the single most valuable test in this slice. Note explicitly
what it does **not** cover: it pins *presence*, not the *role name* — nothing can independently
derive the name, which is why the derivation lives in one place.

### 9.3 One extraction, and it is not optional

`TeamsPage.tsx:353-408` contains the tree flattening with the **orphan-and-cycle rule** (§68's
`buildValueTree` rule): a team whose parent is missing, or two teams pointing at each other, are
surfaced at the root rather than silently dropped. Its own comment explains why that matters —
*"a team the tree does not draw is a team nobody can fix — and the state that most needs fixing is
exactly the one that hides."*

The org chart draws the same tree. **Extract the flattening into `features/admin/teamTree.ts` and
have both screens call it** (T6, behaviour-preserving, its own commit). A copy is how the admin
screen and the org chart come to disagree about which teams exist — and the disagreement would be
invisible on exactly the data that is already broken.

### 9.4 The screen must say that the matrix is the reader's own

The project axis is clipped to `visibleProjects` (§4.4). A reader who cannot see six of the
company's eight projects gets a two-column grid **that looks complete**.

So the panel carries a standing line — *"Only projects you can open are shown here."* — rendered
**unconditionally**, for every reader including a Company Admin who sees everything. Unconditional
because the alternative (rendering it only when something is hidden) is Option D wearing a different
hat: its presence or absence is itself the existence claim §4.3 rejected. It needs no server field,
which is why §5.2's `OrgChart` has no `viewer` block.

### 9.5 Query layer

`useOrgChart()` in `frontend/src/api/queries/admin.ts`, key `keys.orgChart = ['org-chart']` beside
`keys.teams` / `keys.users` / `keys.roleAssignments` (`queries/keys.ts:145-157`).

**Invalidation:** every write that changes staffing must invalidate it — `useGrantRole`,
`useRevokeRole`, `usePutTeamMember`, `useRemoveTeamMember`, `useCreateTeam`, `useUpdateTeam`,
`useDeleteTeam`, `useInviteUser`, `useRemoveUser`. Each of those already invalidates two or three of
`keys.teams` / `keys.roleAssignments` / `keys.projects` / `keys.users`
(`admin.ts:61,87,110-111,139-140,159-160,224,251-253,286-287,307-308`); `keys.orgChart` joins the
list at each. **Missing one is the defect where the chart is stale in exactly the moment somebody
just restaffed a project** — which is the moment they opened it.

## 13. Findings — noticed while planning, **not fixed**

**F1 — a false claim in the contract's own prose.** `docs/specs/openapi.yaml:6678-6681`, in
`CurrentUser.teams`:

> This is the only way a member who is not a Company Admin learns their own teams: `GET /teams` is
> gated on `company:teams`, so the org chart is not readable to the person in it.

`GET /teams` is gated on **`company:read`** (`AdminDirectoryService.java:249` via `readsCompany()`,
`TeamController.java:61-64`). Every member can read the org chart today, which is the opposite of
what this says — and it is the sentence most likely to be read by someone deciding this very
question. It is the §211/§212 class exactly: *prose that names a permission is a claim about the
seeded catalogue, and nothing in this repo checks it.*

**It is not covered by the existing instrument.** `packages/client/src/test/tierClaims.test.ts`
matches `Lead-only` / `Lead and up` and `does not hold` / `not held by` / `withheld from`;
*"gated on `company:teams`"* is none of those forms. Repairing the sentence is folded into **T1**
(it is a contract edit in a slice that already edits the contract). **Extending `tierClaims` to a
"gated on `<key>`" form is a separate slice and is not proposed here** — it is a new rule over four
corpora and would find more than one row.

**F2 — the sentence F1 sits in is also load-bearing for a shipped decision, and survives.**
`CurrentUser.teams` exists so a member learns their own teams. Even with `GET /teams` open to every
member, that field is still the cheaper answer for the app shell, and §175 already removed the one
thing (`isLead`) nothing read. **No change proposed**; recorded so the F1 repair does not turn into
deleting the field.

**F3 — this plan adds a fourth reader of the §A1.1 rule.** Existing: `PermissionEvaluator.projectTerm`
(permissions), `ProjectService.view` (display name), `roleAccess.ts` (client, admits it). §8.3's
extraction keeps the count at three rather than raising it to four. If T2 is skipped or done as a
copy, **that is the defect this slice ships**, and it will not show up in any gate.
