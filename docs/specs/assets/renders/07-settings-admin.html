Settings & administration

Nine screens where a person changes what the system does to other people. Two rules run through all of them. Every setting states its consequence in the same voice as the docs — not what it toggles, what it will cause. And a reader without the permission still sees the screen, in read-only, with a sentence naming who to ask: the rail does not hide these entries, so the page must not be a dead end for whoever follows the link.

Where review applies

Test cases

An approved case cannot be edited without going back through review.

Turning this off leaves 116 cases currently in review approved where they stand. Nothing is discarded.

Documentation pages

A published page needs approval before a new version replaces it.

Off today. Turning it on puts 4 draft pages into review immediately; their authors are notified.

Test plans

A plan must be approved before a run may execute against it.

This is what makes Approve plan a gate rather than a label. Q3 compliance sweep is in draft and would be blocked from running.

Two columns, always the same two

Claim and consequence left, one control right-aligned. Nine settings screens, one scan. A page of mixed inline controls makes a reader hunt for the thing they came to change.

Save is disabled until something changes

The consequence is prose, not a warning icon

Warning colour is reserved for a state that is currently wrong. A consequence is neutral information about a choice not yet made.
