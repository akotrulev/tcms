The rule that decides what lives here

▸
It belongs to the person, not to a project or the company. That single test moves the timesheet and keeps everything else where it was: the rail is scoped by the project switcher or by the company, and a fortnight of a person's hours is scoped by neither.
▸
The brief already stated this rule — its §4.3 uses it to put credentials in the user menu. The timesheet fails the same test the credentials failed; it was simply never asked.
▸
Timesheet approvals is listed for everyone, and the page explains its own refusal. That is the disable-don't-hide rule: hiding it would make a person who does hold Approve time hunt for a row they have, to spare a person who does not one sentence of explanation.
▸
And it carries no count. §8.3's badge table has seven rows and this is the only one that reads none: there is no company-wide "where may I approve" read to ask, and inventing one to feed a badge is a query per sign-in for a number most people will never need. The recorded decision stands.
