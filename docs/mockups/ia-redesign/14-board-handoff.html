Why a shared column and not a "send to" action

proposal §2.2, §2.4 · reversal R7
▸
A card is in one status. Two boards claiming that status show the same card in two places — one object, drawn twice. Nothing is copied, synchronised or notified, so there is no state that can disagree with itself.
▸
Claimed at most once per board stays — it is a unique index, and it is what makes a card's column unambiguous. Only claimed by every board went (R7), which is the half that forced one board to carry another team's columns.
▸
The three hand-offs are the three seams of the line, and each is one drag: PO → developer, developer → tester, tester → PO. Rework is the fourth shared status and runs the other way; Blocked is the fifth and belongs to nobody.
▸
What B2 protected against survives as a warning. A status no board claims is a card nobody sees — the coverage table reports it at the one place boards are configured, instead of a 422 refusing the save.
