Before and after

Company Admin — the role that sees the most, so nothing is hidden by gating

What the left column costs

▸
Up to 36 rows in one scroll, of which 8–12 are project settings. The rail is doing a job a settings section should do.
▸
Two labels appear twice. Staffing is both the project screen and the company one; so is Audit trail. That is the exact ambiguity the Company/Project split exists to prevent.
▸
Mixed taxonomy. Three groups name a subject, two name a scope, and Authoring holds one item.
▸
Collapsed, it is 36 unlabelled icons separated by five rules — the grouping, which is the thing that told you Reports was about execution, is gone.
▸
Two shipped screens are unreachable from it: /board and /run-schedules have no entry and no inbound link from anywhere.

What the right column changes

▸
Three scope zones, fixed order, never mixed — You, Project, Company. The zone rules replace group headings that meant two different things.
▸
The project zone is ordered by how often a screen is opened, not by subject: Work · Test cases · Test plans · Test runs · Releases · Documents, then Overview seventh — it is the project's dashboard, read at stand-up, not the place work is done. No folder stands between a reader and any of them.
▸
Every duplicate label is resolved by naming: Team / Capacity, Project audit / Company audit, Test reports / Analytics.
▸
Both orphans are rescued without costing a row — Run schedules is a Schedules tab on Test runs, and the composite /board is retired into the QA board's card face (Phase 2), linked from Overview until then.
▸
One row leaves the rail entirely. Timesheets is in the user menu with Timesheet approvals beneath it — the rule being that the rail holds places scoped by the switcher or by the company, and a fortnight of one person's hours is scoped by neither (R11).
▸
No route moves. Every path in the screen inventory keeps its URL; only the navigation changes.
